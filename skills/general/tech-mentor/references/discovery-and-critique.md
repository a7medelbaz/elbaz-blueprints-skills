# Discovery and critique

Use a staged interview. Ask one question at a time and stop asking when the answer would not change the recommendation.

## Stages

1. **Outcome:** What real work must the learner perform? Capture target role, task, industry, portfolio result, or deadline outcome.
2. **Context:** Capture deadline, weekly capacity, location/language, budget, device/environment, and work or study constraints.
3. **Baseline:** Capture prior knowledge, projects, transferable skills, evidence, comfort, and unknowns. Do not convert familiarity into competence without a testable signal.
4. **Choice critique:** Test technology, specialization, version, scope, course preferences, and proposed deadline against the outcome and constraints.
5. **Decision checkpoint:** Summarize assumptions, unresolved choices, recommended scope, and the evidence the learner will produce before doing research-heavy design.

Ask a question only when its answer changes scope, sequence, time estimate, version/tool choice, course choice, role recommendation, or required evidence. Otherwise declare an assumption.

## Useful challenges

- Flutter experience may transfer programming and Git habits, but it does not prove Python, HTTP, SQL, Linux, or backend competence. Reuse only what evidence supports and verify the rest.
- “Learn all Odoo modules” is not an actionable target. Narrow by role, business domain, and target workflows.
- “Finish in three months” is not a plan until weekly hours, baseline, and a realistic capability milestone are known.

Critiques should name the learner's choice, concrete risk, evidence or evidence gap, realistic alternative, recommendation, and any decision required.
